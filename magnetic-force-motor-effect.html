Magnetic Force on a Current-Carrying Wire (Motor Effect)

Force on the end-on wire, F = BIL
—
Its direction (Fleming's left-hand rule)
—
Tilted wire: angle to the field θ
—
Tilted wire: F = BIL sinθ
—
End-on view: the wire points straight into or out of the page. Tap the wire to reverse its current.
The field of the magnet (left to right) and the circular field of the current add together. On one side of the wire they point the same way and the field is stronger (field lines crowded); on the other side they oppose and the field is weaker. The wire is pushed from the stronger side towards the weaker side. Check with Fleming's left-hand rule: First finger along the Field, seCond finger along the Current, thuMb gives the Motion. ⊙ = current out of the page, ⊗ = into the page.
Side view: drag the end of the wire to tilt it in the field.
F = BIL sinθ, where θ is the angle between the wire and the field. The force is biggest (F = BIL) when the wire is at 90° to the field, and zero when the wire runs along the field lines. In this view the force points into or out of the page. Notes Example 3: 8.0 cm, 0.15 T, 0.45 A at 90° gives 5.4 × 10-3 N.
