Switch on and watch it run, or grab a side of the coil and turn it by hand. Then untick the commutator and try again.
We look along the axle. The two long sides of the coil carry current in opposite directions (⊙ out of the page, ⊗ into it), so Fleming's left-hand rule pushes one side up and the other down with the same-sized force F = BIL, always at right angles to the field. The pair of forces turns the coil. The turning effect is force × perpendicular distance from the axle, so it is biggest when the coil is horizontal and zero when the coil is vertical — the coil's momentum carries it past that point. At that moment the split-ring commutator swaps the connections, reversing the current in the coil, so the forces keep turning it the same way. Without it, the forces reverse after the vertical position and the coil just rocks back and forth and stops.
