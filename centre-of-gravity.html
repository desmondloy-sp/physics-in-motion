Drag the blue box sideways to tilt it, then let go. Drag the red load to slide it along the top.
  • The dashed purple line drops straight down from the combined centre of gravity: it is the line of action of the weight.
  • While that line passes inside the pivot edge, the weight's moment turns the box back, so it falls back onto its base.
  • Once the centre of gravity passes beyond the pivot edge, the weight's moment turns the box the other way, so it topples.
  • A lower centre of gravity or a wider base makes the toppling angle bigger, so the object is more stable (a coach's luggage bay, a racing car's wide track, a floor fan's heavy base).
  • Try it: put a heavy load on top and watch the toppling angle shrink.
Drag each ball a little way along its surface and let go.
  • Stable: the ball returns to where it was.
  • Unstable: it keeps moving away.
  • Neutral: it stays wherever you leave it.
