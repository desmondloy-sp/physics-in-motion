Drag the rod. Rub it back and forth on the cloth, bring it near the sphere, or touch the sphere with it. Tap the switch on the earth wire to connect or disconnect it.
1. FrictionRub the rod on the cloth until both are charged.
2. ContactTouch the sphere with the charged end of the rod. The sphere gets the same sign as the rod.
3. InductionWithout touching: hold the rod near, connect earth, disconnect earth, then take the rod away. The sphere gets the opposite sign.
Each − is a group of free electrons and each + a group of fixed positive charges. Only electrons move. Rubbing transfers electrons from one insulator to the other — no charge is created, so the rod and cloth always end up equal and opposite. The charge on an insulating rod stays where it was put, so only the charged part that touches the sphere passes its charge on. In the metal sphere the free electrons move easily: a nearby charged rod pushes or pulls them to one side, leaving the other side with an equal and opposite charge. The nearer induced charge is closer to the rod, so the rod attracts even a neutral sphere. An earth wire lets electrons flow to or from the Earth.
