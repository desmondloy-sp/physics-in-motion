Drag the children along the seesaw, or type their distances. Distances are measured from the pivot: negative = left of the pivot.Tutorial A3: where must the 40 kg child B sit to balance the 30 kg child A?
Moment = force × perpendicular distance from the pivot to the line of action of the force. Here each force is a child's weight, W = mg with g = 10 m s-2. A weight on the right of the pivot turns the seesaw clockwise; on the left, anticlockwise. By the principle of moments, the seesaw balances only when the sum of clockwise moments equals the sum of anticlockwise moments. The seesaw starts locked level, so each perpendicular distance is simply the distance along the plank, and the moments are worked out for that position. Remove the lock to see what the seesaw then does: if the moments are not equal, it turns until one end hits the ground. (Once it tilts, the perpendicular distances get shorter, so the moments are no longer shown; moving a child locks it level again.) The seesaw is a light plank pivoted at its centre, so its own weight has no moment. (Tutorial A3 describes a 4.0 m seesaw with the 30 kg child at one end, 2.0 m from the pivot; the plank here is 6 m long so the other scenarios fit, and the answer is the same.)
