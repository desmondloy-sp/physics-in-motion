Drag the children along the seesaw. Tutorial A3: where must the 40 kg child B sit to balance the 30 kg child A?
Moment = force × perpendicular distance from the pivot to the line of action of the force. Here each force is a child's weight, W = mg with g = 10 m s-2, and each distance is measured along the seesaw from the pivot (shown under the beam). A weight on the right of the pivot turns the seesaw clockwise; on the left, anticlockwise. By the principle of moments, the seesaw balances only when the sum of clockwise moments equals the sum of anticlockwise moments. If they are not equal, it turns until one end hits the ground. The seesaw is a light plank pivoted at its centre, so its own weight has no moment. (Tutorial A3 describes a 4.0 m seesaw with the 30 kg child at one end, 2.0 m from the pivot; the plank here is 6 m long so the other presets fit, and the answer, 1.5 m, is the same.)
